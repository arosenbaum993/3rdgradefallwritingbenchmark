Source #1
Growing a School Garden
Students can grow vegetables, flowers, and herbs in a school garden.

1Many schools are adding gardens to their campuses. A school garden can be a place where students grow vegetables, herbs, or flowers while learning about science and nature.

Learning by Doing

2In a garden, students can observe how plants change over time. They can measure plant growth, study insects, compare soil, and record what happens after rain or hot weather. These activities turn lessons into real experiences.

Working Together

3Gardens also give students a chance to work as a team. One group might water plants while another removes weeds or checks for pests. Students must share tools, follow a plan, and complete jobs carefully.

Food and Community

4Some schools use vegetables from their gardens in cooking lessons or donate extra food to families. A garden can also make an empty outdoor space more colorful and useful.

Source #2
School Gardens Need a Plan

1A school garden can be helpful, but it also takes time, money, and planning. Plants need care even when students are busy with other schoolwork.

Daily Work

2Gardens may need watering, weeding, and protection from insects or animals. During very hot weather, plants may need extra water. Teachers must decide who will care for the garden on weekends and during school breaks.

Costs and Supplies

3A garden may require soil, seeds, hoses, gloves, tools, and wood for raised beds. Some schools can get donations, but others must use school money or raise funds.

Choosing the Right Space

4Plants need enough sunlight and a safe place to grow. A garden should not block walkways or playground areas. Schools must also think about students with allergies and make sure everyone can participate safely.

WRITING TASK

Consider what you have learned about school gardens from reviewing the two sources. You will now construct an original text using your own ideas. In your text, answer the research question for the purpose and audience shown below.

Research Question: Should our school create a school garden?

Purpose: to provide and support your opinion about whether your school should create a school garden

Audience: your classmates and teacher

Be sure to clearly state your opinion and give reasons that support it. Use information from both sources to support your reasons. Organize your ideas so they are easy to follow. Use linking words and phrases to connect your reasons and information, and provide a concluding statement or section.
